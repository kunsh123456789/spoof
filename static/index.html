    Will it keep working with the Mac asleep or the lid closed?

    Not while the Mac is truly asleep — and no app can change that. On iOS 17 and later the fake location only exists while this Mac holds the connection to the phone open; a sleeping Mac holds nothing, so the phone goes back to its real GPS until the Mac wakes (then the app restores it).

    What the app does: stops the Mac from falling asleep by itself whenever it is needed (the screen can still go dark).

    What it cannot do: closing the lid of a MacBook on battery puts it to sleep no matter what. To run with the lid shut, plug in power and use an external display with a keyboard or mouse (“clamshell mode”) — or simply leave the lid open and turn the brightness down. It also cannot wake a sleeping Mac for a scheduled step, so leave the Mac awake before the plan's first step.

    iPhones on iOS 16 and earlier keep their fake location by themselves, so only a running trip needs the Mac awake.

    Wi-Fi setup (no cable)
    1. Plug the iPhone in once, tap Trust, and turn on Developer Mode.
    2. Switch on Allow connecting over Wi-Fi above (needs the cable).
    3. Put the Mac and iPhone on the same Wi-Fi network and keep the iPhone awake and unlocked.
    4. Unplug. Within a few seconds the phone shows a Wi-Fi badge. If not, press Find Wi-Fi phones. On a Mac you may also need Finder → your iPhone → Show this iPhone when on Wi-Fi.

    Wi-Fi is newer than the cable and can be less reliable; iOS 17.0–17.3 over Wi-Fi may need the tunneld command from the README. Controlling two iOS 17+ phones at once also needs tunneld.

          How it works
          1. Press New plan, give it a name and a date, then add steps: each has a clock time and an action (start trick-or-treat, walk somewhere, run a saved route, set a location, stop, or restore real GPS).
          2. Press Save plan, then switch Activate on. A plan never runs unless it is activated.
          3. When the real clock reaches a step's time, the app does it by itself, one step after another.
          4. The app must be open, the Mac awake and the phone connected at that time. The app keeps the Mac awake for you (see Phones & connection → Keep Mac awake).
          5. Instead of a clock time, a step can say After the last move: it waits until the previous step is finished and the phone has stopped moving, then runs after the number of seconds or minutes you type (the countdown starts over if the phone moves again).
          6. If it was late (the Mac slept), a step still runs up to the plan's late limit (default 10 min); after that it is marked missed instead of surprising you later.

          Shaky = GPS noise plus a natural, uneven pace for that walk only. ⏵ Test runs a single step right now, to check it works.

                How it works
                1. While you have internet, zoom the map so the neighborhood you'll use fills the screen.
                2. Give it a name and press Download what I see. The app saves the map, the houses and the streets for that area on your Mac (a few MB).
                3. Later with no data, everything works inside that area: the map still shows, Find houses uses the saved houses, and walking/driving paths follow the saved streets.
                4. The green box on the map shows exactly what will be saved (the part of the map not hidden behind the side card). Dashed boxes show areas already saved.

                For trick-or-treat, make sure your whole neighborhood fits on screen (up to about 2.4 × 2.4 km). Map zoom levels 12–17 are saved; zooming in further offline shows blank tiles.
